Speak up in a group discussion without fear

A GD isn't a fight to be the loudest. Evaluators look for clear thinking, good listening and the person who moves the discussion forward.

hearingListen firstlightbulbAdd valueflagLead politely
visibility

What evaluators look for

lightbulb

Content

Relevant points, facts and examples.

record_voice_over

Communication

Clear, simple, confident speech.

hearing

Listening

Building on others' points, not ignoring them.

flag

Leadership

Starting, guiding or summarising, without dominating.

handshake

Teamwork

Including quiet members and staying respectful.

play_arrow

Ways to start the GD

  • check_circleWith a definition: "Let's first understand what remote work means for freshers..."
  • check_circleWith a question: "Will AI take more jobs than it creates? Let's look at both sides."
  • check_circleWith an example from India: UPI, ISRO's missions, startup growth.
  • check_circleWith a fact, but only if you're sure it's correct.
lightbulb
Starting gets attention, but only if your opening is good. If you're unsure, make the second or third point strongly instead.
chat

Phrases that help

Agree"I agree with Ravi, and I'd like to add one more point..."
Disagree"I see it a little differently. In my view..."
Add value"Building on that idea, let's also consider..."
Bring back"We seem to be going off-topic. Can we come back to...?"
Include others"Priya, we haven't heard your view yet. What do you think?"
Conclude"To sum up, we discussed three points..."

Do's and don'ts

thumb_upDo

  • check_circleMake eye contact with everyone, not only the evaluator
  • check_circleSpeak 3–4 times with quality points
  • check_circleNote down points while others speak
  • check_circleStay calm if someone interrupts

thumb_downDon't

  • cancelShout or speak over others
  • cancelStay silent the whole time
  • cancelPoint fingers or get personal
